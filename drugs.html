Drugs

Treatments and what they mean for ε4/ε4

APOE genotype matters most for the safety of anti-amyloid antibodies. Several newer approaches target APOE4 biology directly. Within each group, the most advanced or most recent come first.

Approved for early Alzheimer’s

DrugTypeFor ε4/ε4
Lecanemab (Leqembi)Eisai and BiogenSee trialFDA label ↗
Anti-amyloid antibody; IV, with a weekly under-the-skin autoinjector (Leqembi Iqlik) approved in the US in 2025 for maintenance after 18 months
Higher risk of ARIA, including symptomatic and serious ARIA, in ε4/ε4. In the US, a boxed warning applies and APOE testing is advised before treatment; patients who decline testing can still be treated. In the EU (April 2025), approved only for people who are not ε4 homozygotes.1,2
Donanemab (Kisunla)Eli LillySee trialFDA label ↗
Anti-amyloid antibody, IV
Higher risk of ARIA in ε4/ε4. In the US, a boxed warning applies, APOE testing is advised before treatment, and extra vigilance for ARIA is advised in the first 24 weeks. The FDA approved a slower dose titration that lowered ARIA-E, most in ε4/ε4 (TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 6). In the EU (September 2025), approved only for people who are not ε4 homozygotes.3,4,5
Donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamineGeneric; three separate drugsSource ↗
Cholinesterase inhibitors: boost acetylcholine, a memory chemical messenger
Treat symptoms of mild to moderate Alzheimer’s (donepezil also severe); do not slow the disease. Used regardless of APOE genotype.6
MemantineGenericSource ↗
NMDA receptor antagonist: regulates glutamate signalling
Treats symptoms of moderate to severe Alzheimer’s, alone or with donepezil; does not slow the disease. Used regardless of APOE genotype.6

DrugTypeFor ε4/ε4

Oral; blocks formation of toxic amyloid oligomers
Phase 3 APOLLOE4 in ε4/ε4 missed its primary endpoint; a prespecified MCI subgroup favored drug. Low ARIA.7

Oral LRRK2 inhibitor acting on the RAB10 pathway
Phase 2a prevention trial in biomarker-positive ε4 carriers, now recruiting in Abu Dhabi, UAE.

Phase 1/2 in ε4/ε4; early biomarker signals, no ARIA reported. FDA Fast Track.8

Earlier-stage approaches

DrugTypeFor ε4/ε4
Anti-APOE antibodiesSource ↗
Antibodies against aggregated apoE, such as HAE-4
Lower amyloid plaques in APOE4 mice; not yet in clinical trials as far as published reports show.9
APOE4 loweringSource ↗
Antisense oligonucleotides and gene silencing
Aim to reduce APOE production; lowering APOE before plaques form reduced amyloid in mice. Preclinical or early development.10
APOE2 plus APOE4 silencingSource ↗
Gene therapy (for example Lexeo’s LX1020)
Adds protective APOE2 while suppressing APOE4; preclinical.11
Gene editingSource ↗
CRISPR-based conversion of APOE4
Proof-of-concept studies only, such as prime editing of APOE4 in mice with brain-directed AAV; efficiency must improve before clinical use.12

Are lecanemab and donanemab approved for ε4/ε4?

Whether a person with two copies of APOE4 can be prescribed either anti-amyloid antibody depends on the country. Where approved, it is with a boxed warning about the higher risk of ARIA; where not, approval covers only people with zero or one copy.

In the US, APOE testing is advised before treatment but not required; patients who decline testing can still be treated, without knowing their ARIA risk. Where the drugs are not approved for ε4/ε4, testing is effectively needed to establish eligibility. Confirmed amyloid on a PET scan or spinal fluid test is required everywhere.15,16,5

Cost in the US

  • Lecanemab: about $26,500 a year by IV; the weekly autoinjector for maintenance is about $19,500 a year.17
  • Donanemab: about $12,500 for 6 months up to about $48,900 for 18 months, since treatment can stop once amyloid clears.18
  • Medicare: covered under Part B with 20% coinsurance and no annual out-of-pocket cap, over $5,000 a year for lecanemab at list price. Amyloid testing and repeated MRI scans add to the cost, more so for ε4/ε4.19
