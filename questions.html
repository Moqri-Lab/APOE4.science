Open questions

What research still needs to answer

Gaps where new data on ε4/ε4 carriers could change prevention and treatment.

  1. What changes first?

    Plaque amyloid, amyloid in vessel walls, small bleeds, blood–brain barrier leakage and tau all rise in ε4/ε4 carriers. Their order before symptoms is not known.

  2. What sets the timing?

    Onset age still varies by a decade or more. Biological aging, measured with blood-based aging clocks, is one candidate explanation.

  3. Who is protected?

    Some ε4/ε4 carriers stay well into their eighties. Genetic and biological modifiers of risk could point to new treatment targets.

  4. How can prevention trials be made safe and fast?

    Natural history data define realistic outcomes, sample sizes and timing, and help identify who is most at risk of side effects such as ARIA.

  5. Does the effect differ by ancestry and sex?

    The ε4 risk is lower on average in people of African ancestry and differs between women and men. Most ε4/ε4 data still come from people of European ancestry.
