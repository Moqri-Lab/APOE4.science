If you carry ε4/ε4

What the genotype means, and what it does not

Learning you have two copies of APOE4 can be hard news. It is a strong risk factor, not a diagnosis. Risk is high, but the age when changes begin varies, and research on prevention is moving fast.

Confirm and discuss

Consumer test results should be confirmed with a clinical test. A doctor or genetic counselor can explain what the result means for you and your family.

Ask about treatment safety

Anti-amyloid drugs for early Alzheimer’s carry a higher risk of brain swelling and bleeding (ARIA) in ε4/ε4 carriers. See the Drugs page.

Look after heart and brain health

APOE4 also affects cholesterol and the blood vessels. Blood pressure, cholesterol, hearing, sleep and physical activity are worth discussing with your doctor.

Consider taking part in research

Registries match volunteers with studies, some of which enroll ε4 carriers specifically: Alzheimer’s Prevention Registry and GeneMatch, Alzheimer’s Association TrialMatch, and ClinicalTrials.gov. APOE4.Info is a peer community of carriers. See also the Trials page.

This site is for education and is not medical advice. Talk with your doctor or a genetic counselor about your own situation.
