The gene

One letter apart

Apolipoprotein E carries cholesterol and other lipids in the brain and blood. Everyone inherits two copies, one from each parent.

AlleleResidue 112Residue 158Alzheimer’s risk
ε2CCysCCysLower than ε3
ε3CCysRArgReference (most common)
ε4RArgRArgHighest; strongest with two copies

A single amino-acid change at residue 112 separates ε4 from ε3. Carrying one ε4 copy raises risk; carrying two (ε4/ε4, homozygous) raises it much further and shifts symptoms to an earlier, more predictable age. Risk by genotype also differs by age, sex and ancestry.

What we know

Evidence so far

~65

Typical age at symptom onset in ε4/ε4 carriers, with a narrower range than in ε3/ε3. Almost all show Alzheimer’s pathology at autopsy.

Fortea et al., Nature Medicine 2024 (NACC brain donors and five biomarker cohorts)

55

Age from which Alzheimer’s biomarkers are already higher than in ε3/ε3. By 65, nearly all have abnormal spinal-fluid amyloid and about 75% have a positive amyloid scan.

Fortea et al., Nature Medicine 2024

~13×

Higher odds of moderate to severe amyloid in brain blood vessel walls (cerebral amyloid angiopathy) at autopsy with two ε4 copies.

Greenberg et al., Annals of Neurology 1995

4.8×

Higher odds of accumulating two or more small brain bleeds over follow-up in ε4/ε4 adults without symptoms. This matters for the safety of anti-amyloid drugs.

A4 Study, Alzheimer’s & Dementia 2025
