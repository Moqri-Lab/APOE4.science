A resource for carriers and researchers

Two copies of APOE4 may be a genetic form of Alzheimer’s disease.

Plain-language background for people with the ε4/ε4 genotype, and the evidence, data and open questions for the scientists studying it.

ε4/ε4About 2–3% of people of European ancestry

The gene

One letter apart

Apolipoprotein E carries cholesterol and other lipids in the brain and blood. It comes in three common forms that differ at just two positions in the protein, residues 112 and 158. Everyone inherits two copies, one from each parent.

AlleleResidue 112Residue 158Alzheimer’s risk
ε2CCysCCysLower than ε3
ε3CCysRArgReference (most common)
ε4RArgRArgHighest; strongest with two copies

A single amino-acid change at residue 112 separates ε4 from ε3. Carrying one ε4 copy raises risk; carrying two (ε4/ε4, homozygous) raises it much further and shifts symptoms to an earlier, more predictable age.

What we know

Evidence so far

~65

Typical age at symptom onset in ε4/ε4 carriers, with a narrower range than in ε3/ε3. Almost all show Alzheimer’s pathology at autopsy.

Fortea et al., Nature Medicine 2024 (NACC brain donors and five biomarker cohorts)

55

Age from which Alzheimer’s biomarkers are already higher than in ε3/ε3. By 65, nearly all have abnormal spinal-fluid amyloid and about 75% have a positive amyloid scan.

Fortea et al., Nature Medicine 2024

~13×

Higher odds of moderate to severe amyloid in brain blood vessel walls (cerebral amyloid angiopathy) at autopsy with two ε4 copies.

Greenberg et al., Annals of Neurology 1995

4.8×

Higher odds of accumulating two or more small brain bleeds over follow-up in ε4/ε4 adults without symptoms. This matters for the safety of anti-amyloid drugs, which carry a boxed warning for ε4 homozygotes.

A4 Study, Alzheimer’s & Dementia 2025

If you carry ε4/ε4

What the genotype means, and what it does not

Learning you have two copies of APOE4 can be hard news. It is a strong risk factor, not a diagnosis. Risk is high, but the age when changes begin varies, and research on prevention is moving fast.

Confirm and discuss

Consumer test results should be confirmed with a clinical test. A doctor or genetic counselor can explain what the result means for you and your family.

Ask about treatment safety

Anti-amyloid drugs for early Alzheimer’s (lecanemab, donanemab) carry a higher risk of brain swelling and bleeding (ARIA) in ε4/ε4 carriers. Their labels advise APOE testing before treatment.

Look after heart and brain health

APOE4 also affects cholesterol and the blood vessels. Blood pressure, cholesterol, hearing, sleep and physical activity are worth discussing with your doctor.

Consider taking part in research

Registries match volunteers with studies, some of which enroll ε4 carriers specifically: Alzheimer’s Prevention Registry and GeneMatch, Alzheimer’s Association TrialMatch, and ClinicalTrials.gov. APOE4.Info is a peer community of carriers.

This site is for education and is not medical advice. Talk with your doctor or a genetic counselor about your own situation.

Open questions

What research still needs to answer

  1. What changes first?

    Plaque amyloid, amyloid in vessel walls, small bleeds, blood–brain barrier leakage and tau all rise in ε4/ε4 carriers. Their order before symptoms is not known.

  2. What sets the timing?

    Onset age still varies by a decade or more. Biological aging, measured with blood-based aging clocks, is one candidate explanation.

  3. Who is protected?

    Some ε4/ε4 carriers stay well into their eighties. Genetic and biological modifiers of risk could point to new treatment targets.

  4. How can prevention trials be made safe and fast?

    Natural history data define realistic outcomes, sample sizes and timing, and help identify who is most at risk of side effects such as ARIA.
